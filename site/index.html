Trueline

Measure a room. Price it. Get it signed.

Walk a room with an iPhone and the walls, the doors and the windows come back as numbers. Put a tape on one wall running each way, and the takeoff, the price, the proposal and the insurance document all come out of the same measurements — so they cannot disagree with each other.

The one thing it will not do

Tell you a scan is a measurement.

Every length in this app carries where it came from, and the words are on the drawing. A length the phone found is Scanned. A length you put a tape on is Measured. A room drawn on a grid says Drawn. Until a tape has been on one wall running each way, every document says so, on its face, where a client will read it.

That is the whole product. A number you cannot defend to a homeowner line by line is worse than no number.

How a job goes through it

  1. Walk the room

    The phone finds the walls, the doors and the windows. No LiDAR? Tap the corners onto a grid, or point at each one through the camera.

  2. Put a tape on it

    One wall running each way is enough. The app tells you which walls are worth it and why — an error on the longest wall costs the most floor.

  3. Take it off

    Floor, ceiling, wall face, baseboard, framing, every door and window taken off. The arithmetic is shown, not hidden.

  4. Price it from your own book

    Your rates, typed once. No averages, no market data, nothing guessing what drywall costs where you are. Every line is a number you set times a number the room measured.

  5. Get it signed

    A proposal in sentences, what is not included, and somewhere to sign — with a record of who signed, when, the exact words they agreed to, and a fingerprint of the document.

  6. Bill against what was signed

    Deposits, progress payments and the final, all built from the signed scope. Anything that changes becomes a change order, signed before it starts.

What is in it

The drawing

A plan with real dimension lines, doors, windows, north, and your letterhead on it. Blueprint or 3D — walk through the room, or take the roof off the whole floor.

The takeoff

Every quantity, with its workings, as a sheet you can send, copy, or open in a spreadsheet.

Insurance

Mark the damage while you are standing in front of it. Photographs, moisture readings over time, a claim document, and ESX for Xactimate.

Scheduling and invoicing

Days into your own calendar. Invoices off the signed scope. A QuickBooks export. No monthly service behind any of it.

Your business, once

Licence number, insurance, logo and rates typed one time and on every document after that.

Written on the phone

On an iPhone that supports Apple Intelligence, the app will draft the scope paragraph and the loss description from the figures it already has — free, offline, and nothing leaves the device. You read every word before it goes out.

Where your work lives

ThingWhere it isWhat that means
The roomsA folder per scan on your phoneVisible in the Files app. AirDrop one, mail one, copy one off. No account needed to read your own work.
The backupYour own iCloudNot ours — yours. A corrected room is about 6 kB, so a free iCloud account holds a very large number of them.
The photographsOn the phoneA scan's pictures run to tens of megabytes. Sending them up would fill your iCloud with one job, so that is a decision per job rather than a default.
Everything elseNowhereThere is no Trueline server. Nothing is uploaded, nothing is analysed, and there is no way for one person's house to reach another's.
