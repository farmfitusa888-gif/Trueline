What ScanToBid Should Cost

Every platform in this market charges by the seat and every scanner charges by the scan. The second one is repeatedly called out as punishing exactly the contractor this is built for. That is the opening.

Read the middle column and the wedge is obvious. There is nothing between $33 and $49, and the $33 is a scanner that stops at the drawing. Everything that does proposals and change orders starts at $149.

ScanToBid does what the $33 tool does, plus the two things a contractor actually buys the $149 tool for: a proposal a homeowner can sign, and a scope that freezes when they do. It does not do scheduling, invoicing, crew management or a CRM — and pricing it as though it did would be the fastest way to lose to a product that does.

Why $98, and why an earlier draft of this page was wrong

This document first said $29, anchored against magicplan at ~$33. That was the wrong comparison and it was about a third of honest. ScanToBid is not a scanner. It measures, takes off, prices, proposes, takes a signature, freezes the scope, raises change orders, schedules the work and invoices for it — which is the estimating half of Jobber and JobTread plus measurement neither of them has at all.

$98 is 34% below Jobber's $149 and 55% below JobTread once its per-user charge is counted. You are the value choice against products that do less of what a remodeler actually needs, rather than the cheap choice against a scanner.

It is under a hundred, which is a real line for a contractor signing up on a phone, and it leaves room to discount without devaluing — which is what the founding rate below is for.

And it is one number. No per-scan fee and no per-project fee, ever — the single loudest complaint in this market, and the one thing every competitor does. Say it on the pricing page in those words.

Why the free tier is not generosity

It is doing three jobs at once, and dropping it would cost you all three.

It is what gets you through App Store review. The rejection that matters here is Guideline 4.2 — an app that is a login wall with no standalone value. A free tier that genuinely measures a room is not that.

It is the fallback if review ever goes badly. Slack, Notion and Figma all ship a free app and sell the plan on the web. That route only exists if the free app is genuinely useful on its own.

And it is how a contractor tries it. Nobody in this trade subscribes to a measuring app they have not stood in a room with. Let him measure his own kitchen for nothing, then charge him the first time he wants a quantity out of it.

The line moved while this was being written. It used to include the takeoff, which was giving away the product: the drawing is the demonstration and the quantities are what people pay for. Free now stops at the floor area.

What has to be built before you can charge

None of this exists yet. In the order it has to happen.

  1. StoreKit 2, and a paywall that is honest about what it gates

    Two products in App Store Connect — a monthly and a yearly auto-renewing subscription — and the code that reads whether the person has one. Apple gives you the receipt validation; you do not need a server for it.

  2. The gate itself, in one place

    A single check the whole app asks, rather than a paywall scattered through every screen. Measuring, the plan and the takeoff never ask it. Proposals, signatures and exports do.

  3. A free tier that cannot be mistaken for a broken one

    When a locked thing is tapped, say what it does and what it costs — not "upgrade to unlock". A contractor who has just measured his kitchen has earned a straight sentence.

  4. Restore purchases, and the family case

    Apple requires a restore path. It is small and it is a rejection if it is missing.

What I am not certain of

  • No price in the table was quoted to me. All of it comes from trade comparison articles, several published by competitors of the products they compare. Right order of magnitude, not quotes.
  • $29 is a judgement, not a finding. No contractor has been asked what they would pay for this. The reasoning above is the best I can do without that, and one afternoon of asking would beat it.
  • The revenue lines are arithmetic, not a forecast. They are what N subscribers produce. Nothing here predicts N.
  • I have not read Apple's current agreement. The 15% is the Small Business Program rate as reported; confirm it against your own App Store Connect account before relying on it.
