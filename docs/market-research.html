The As-Built Gap

Every scanning app on the market sells you a scan. None of them sells a measured building that a human has signed off on. That is the whole opening.

Interior reality capture·Remodelers & general contractors·iOS LiDAR

01Photos alone will not measure a room

The ask was “take pictures and get measurements out of them.” The measuring half is the hard half, and the capture technology decides whether it is possible at all.

Accuracy on interior walls — published figures, none measured here

Photogrammetry fails indoors for a reason that cannot be engineered away: plain painted drywall has no texture for the solver to match against. It wins on small objects and organic shapes. It loses on flat interior surfaces — which is the entire subject of a floor plan.

The product, in one line

Depth capture measures. The human’s typed number overrides. Photos are the visual record and the evidence — never the ruler.

02This cannot be a website

WebXR handheld AR is still unsupported in Safari on iPhone and iPad in 2026. Depth sensing, plane detection and mesh detection have limited or no support, and there is no browser path to LiDAR at all. The Depth Sensing Module remains a W3C Working Draft.

Workarounds exist — 8th Wall runs its own SLAM on the camera feed inside stock Safari, Variant Launch injects a real WebXR API through App Clips — but none of them reach LiDAR, and LiDAR is where the accuracy lives.

So: a native iOS scanner, and the web for everything else. Projects, the 2D and 3D viewer, the builder, accounts, sharing and PDF output all run fine in a browser on any tablet or desktop.

03Apple gives a free head start. Android does not.

Apple’s RoomPlan returns a parametric room — walls, doors, windows, openings — not just a point cloud docs. Android’s ARCore Depth API reaches over 87% of active devices and needs no time-of-flight sensor, but returns raw depth only. There is no RoomPlan equivalent on Android; the open feature request on Google’s SDK has no ship date.

Android is therefore not “the same app on another platform.” It is: build wall-plane fitting, corner solving and opening detection from scratch. Defer it — and design the data format so it can arrive later without a rewrite.

RoomPlan’s real limits — engineer around these, not away from them

  • Walls are modelled at a uniform ~16 cm thickness regardless of reality; anything over ~50 cm splits into two thin walls. Handling improved in iOS 18.
  • Only a fixed set of common household objects is recognised.
  • Double doors and door/window combinations merge incorrectly.
  • Max scan area ~9 × 9 m (30 × 30 ft); longer sessions drift, overheat and degrade tracking. A session should not exceed five minutes.
  • Each room arrives in its own coordinate system, and Apple’s own merge is limited to one floor, one horizontal plane docs.
  • Merging simplifies geometry — it optimises how the model looks at the expense of how accurate it is.
Read that last cluster again

Multi-room and multi-storey stitching is exactly where every consumer scanner visibly falls apart — and exactly what “multiple rooms, houses, buildings” demands. Houses have stairs. Apple’s merge stops at one floor.

Matterport’s own positioning says its AI floor plans are “not CAD-accurate drawings and should not be used for construction or renovation planning.”

The biggest name in the category explicitly disclaims the exact job your friend needs done.

Two structural facts

  • Nobody owns interiors + accuracy + estimating together. Hover owns exteriors. magicplan owns insurance restoration. Polycam owns raw 3D. Canvas owns CAD by paying humans. CubiCasa owns real-estate volume. The remodeler doing interior as-builts to price a job is served by pieces of all of them and the whole of none.
  • Almost everyone meters per scan. Reviews are blunt that this “punishes low-volume contractors” — under ten jobs a month the math stops working. A flat seat price is a real weapon here.

05What users complain about

Collected from app-store review aggregators and review sites. Reported complaints, not my testing.

Polycam

  • Pricing structure, “perceived misleading free trials”, unexpected charges.
  • Dimensions and floor-plan detail locked behind higher tiers — users call these “essential to using the application.”
  • Crashes and processing failures; scans stopping mid-capture; the app closing without producing a plan.
  • Users reported losing hundreds of scans after upgrading, with no remedy.

magicplan

  • Subscription cost called prohibitive for independents; unclear ROI for infrequent users.
  • Mixed accuracy — some report solid results and reliable door/window detection, others report discrepancies that propagate into layout errors.

Floor-plan tools generally

  • “The biggest frustration is export limitations, not design features.” The tool looks great until you need to hand something to a client.
  • Missing roles and permissions, version control, and communication tools.
  • Seat limits push teams into sharing credentials — a security problem and a growth blocker.

And the workflow itself

  • Professionals spend 8 to 24 hours measuring one residential space.
  • Miss one measurement and it is another trip to site.
  • Teams trust decades-old CAD files that are “artifacts, not reality” — no undocumented renovations, no structural sag.
  • Errors stay hidden until framing or equipment placement, then get paid for through RFIs, redesign, rework and coordination churn.

06What they wish they had

Each line below is a feature we can build, derived from the complaints above and the technical limits in §03.

  1. A correction layer that re-solves. Type the real number for one wall and every connected room, corner, opening and quantity updates around it. Existing apps let you edit a value; they do not propagate it. Highest-value differentiator by a distance.
  2. Multi-room and multi-storey stitching that survives. Corner-anchored capture plus loop closure — the known SLAM answer to accumulated drift.
  3. Dimensions never paywalled. The thing Polycam users are angriest about.
  4. Exports that keep their dimensions. magicplan’s own help page states DXF export to SketchUp/AutoCAD does not include dimensions. Small engineering job, large sales line.
  5. Real roles and permissions, and enough seats that nobody shares a password.
  6. Version history on a plan — which measurement changed, who changed it, when, and what it was before. Nobody offers it; every remodeler needs it the day a client disputes a number.
  7. Flat pricing. No per-scan meter.
  8. Offline capture in a basement, syncing when signal returns.
  9. Wall thickness that is real — not a uniform 16 cm — with a fast way to say “these are 2×4, these are 2×6, that one is masonry.”
  10. Confidence shown honestly. Mark every dimension as scanned (±) or human-verified (exact). Someone ordering cabinets needs to know which is which. No competitor does this and it is nearly free to build.

07Ideas past the edge of the field

  • Scan → scope → price. Areas, wall runs, opening counts and ceiling heights fall out of the model free. Turn them into quantities: drywall SF, paint SF less openings, flooring SF plus waste, baseboard LF. This is the bridge to Plumbline and the reason the thing pays for itself. Prices come from the company’s own accepted-estimate history — never invented.
  • A verification punch list. After a scan, the app names the six dimensions it is least sure about and asks for a tape check on those. Turns 8–24 hours of measuring into a 20-minute walk plus six checks.
  • Before/after overlay. Re-scan mid-job and diff against the as-built. Shows the client what changed; catches framing that went in wrong.
  • Anchors that survive. Drop a persistent marker at a corner so next week’s re-scan lands in the same coordinate system instead of drifting into a new one.
  • Photo pins in 3D. Every photo attaches to the point it was taken from, so “the outlet behind the fridge” is findable six weeks later.
  • Hidden conditions marked in space — joist runs, the stack, the knob-and-tube they found — pinned in the model, not buried in a note.
  • A client link with a 3D walkthrough and no login. Matterport’s entire business is this one feature.
  • Xactimate ESX export, if he touches insurance restoration at all. It is magicplan’s strongest moat and the reason restoration contractors pay $33 a month.
  • Exteriors later. Hover proves photos-only works outside, where brick, siding and shingles give the solver the texture drywall never will.
  • AI writes words, never numbers. A drafted scope of work off the model — every figure in it from geometry or SQL, never from the model’s imagination.

08Where this wins

Everyone else sells a scan. This sells a measured building a human has signed off on — one that gets more accurate every time somebody checks a number, and that turns into a priced scope.

The wedge, most defensible first

  1. The correction layer — typed exact measurements that re-solve the model.
  2. Stitching that holds across rooms and floors, where Apple’s own merge gives up.
  3. Confidence marking — scanned vs. verified, visible on every dimension.
  4. Flat seat pricing against a market that meters per scan.
  5. Scan → priced scope, through the seam to Plumbline.
