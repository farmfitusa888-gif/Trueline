# V3 — what to build once V2 is in contractors' hands

V1 is one room done properly. V2 is more than one room, priced, and out the door
to a client. This is what comes after, and it is written now rather than later
because three of the four ideas below change what should be *stored* today, and
storage decided badly is the one thing that is expensive to undo.

Ordered by what a remodeler would pay for, not by what is interesting to build.

---

## 1. Scan → priced scope (the one that pays for the product)

Already half here. The takeoff produces quantities, the price book turns them
into money, and job outcomes will eventually say what this contractor really
charges. What is missing is the step between a number and a **scope of work** —
the document that says *what will be done*, not just what it costs.

**What it is.** "Remove existing base throughout (78 lf). Hang and finish 460 sq
ft of 1/2 in board. Two coats over primer. Supply and hang one 3/0 pre-hung
interior door, 4 9/16 jamb." Every figure in that sentence already exists in the
model. The only thing being added is English.

**The rule that must not bend.** AI writes the language and never the numbers.
Every quantity, size and count is substituted in from the geometry; nothing is
generated that a person could mistake for a measurement. If the provider is off
or unavailable, the scope still comes out — plainer, from templates — because a
contractor standing in a driveway cannot wait for an API.

**What to store now:** nothing new. This reads what is already there.

**Why it is first:** it turns a twenty-minute scan into a sendable quote, which
is the whole promise on the pricing page. Everything else on this list makes the
product better; this is the thing it is *for*.

---

## 2. The client link, hosted (the first thing worth charging extra for)

The self-contained file already ships and covers most of it. What a hosted
version adds is worth money on its own:

- **It stays current.** Change a measurement and the client is looking at the
  new one, rather than at the file you sent on Tuesday.
- **It can be taken back.** A job that goes sour should not leave a permanent
  copy of somebody's house in a text thread.
- **You can see it was opened.** "Did you get the quote?" answered without
  asking.

**What it costs:** a server, from the day it is turned on. That is the whole
reason it is not built. It should be priced as its own tier, not absorbed —
see `docs/money.md`.

**What to store now:** the client file is already built from a pure function of
`(room, company, takeoff, quote, photos)`. Keep it that way and hosting is a
wrapper around the same call, not a rewrite.

## 3. Re-scan and diff — before, after, and what actually got built

**What it is.** Scan the room again mid-job. The app shows what changed: this
wall moved 2 inches, this opening is 4 inches narrower than it was framed to be,
this partition is not where it was drawn.

**Why a remodeler cares**, in the order it costs them money:

1. **Framing that went in wrong is found while it is still cheap.** A wall an
   inch off is a cabinet run that does not fit, discovered on delivery day.
2. **A change order has evidence.** "You asked for the opening widened" is a
   different conversation with two scans and a date on each.
3. **Progress is showable.** The client sees the week.

**The hard part is not the diff, it is the frame.** Two scans of the same room
arrive in different coordinate systems, and everything in this product depends
on knowing which wall is which. The honest answer is the same one that made
stitching work: **a person says**. Tap a wall in the old scan, tap the same wall
in the new one, and the rest is arithmetic — the identical trick `floor.ts` uses,
against the same exactness guarantees.

**What to store now, and this one matters:** a room already keeps `id` and every
wall keeps an `id`, and the importer keeps `sourceIds` mapping them back to
RoomPlan's own UUIDs. **Do not lose `sourceIds`.** It is the only thread between
two captures of the same building, and it is already being written.

## 4. Exteriors — roof, siding, the whole property

Photogrammetry fails indoors for a specific unfixable reason: plain painted
drywall has no texture for a solver to match. **Outdoors it works** — brick,
siding, shingles, a sky edge. Hover has built a business on exactly that and
charges $99/month plus $29–$139 a project.

**Biggest market, biggest build, and the least shared with everything above.**
It is a different capture, a different solver, and a different set of quantities.
Worth wanting; not worth starting until interiors have paying customers.

**What to store now:** nothing. But do not let the model assume a building is one
horizontal floor plane, and it currently does not — `floor.ts` is explicit that
multiple storeys are a separate problem rather than a missing feature.

## Ideas that are smaller than a bet, and each worth a week

Ordered by value per day of work.

**Anchors that survive.** Drop a marker at a corner so a re-scan next week lands
in the same coordinate system instead of drifting into a new one. Makes idea 3
one tap instead of two, and makes multi-scan stitching of a large room possible.

**The verification punch list on the lock screen.** The app already knows which
two walls are worth a tape. Putting that on a notification when somebody arrives
at a job is the difference between a feature and a habit.

**Hidden conditions, pinned in space.** Where the joists run, where the stack is,
where they found knob-and-tube. Not a note file — a pin in the 3D view, at the
spot, with a photograph. Nobody in the field offers this and every remodeler has
a phone full of photographs they cannot find again.

**A cut list.** The framing quantities are already there — plates, studs, headers.
Turning them into a stock list with lengths and a cutting order is arithmetic on
numbers this app already has.

**Two-scanner agreement as a feature, not a test.** Sam scanned his garage with
two apps and they disagreed by 6.2 sq ft. Scan a room twice with *this* app and
show the difference: it is the only honest measure of how much a scan should be
trusted, and no competitor can copy it without admitting theirs vary too.

**Voice notes keyed to a wall.** Tap a wall, hold, talk. Transcribed later. What
a contractor actually does on site is talk, not type.

**A "what changed" line on every export.** Every measurement already carries
`supersedes`. A one-line history under a disputed number — *"was 11' 7", changed
to 11' 9" by Gilbert on 14 March, tape"* — is a feature nobody in this market has
and the model has been carrying the data for since the first week.

---

## What would make this the best app in its class

Not a feature. The category's whole business is selling a scan and letting the
buyer assume it is a measurement. This one says out loud that a scan is a guess
until somebody checks it, tells you exactly which two walls to check, and then
marks every number for the rest of its life with who stood behind it.

Everything above should be judged against that sentence. A feature that makes
the app more useful and blurs it is a bad trade — including, especially, anything
that makes an AI-written number look like a measured one.
