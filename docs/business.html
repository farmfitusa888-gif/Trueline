What it takes to make this a business

Every competitor price, feature and user complaint on this page is sourced from MARKET-RESEARCH.md, which was researched on 2026-08-19 and labels its own provenance. Everything about the product is read out of the code. Everything else — conversion, churn, what a customer costs to get — is an assumption, marked as one, because nobody has sold this to anybody yet.

You asked for the version that shows where it fails. That is section 05, and the dials are live: move them until it stops working and you will see how little has to go wrong.

01

What is actually built

Not a roadmap. This is what compiles, passes 788 tests and 18 audit parts, and ran on a phone this week — where the first real afternoon with it found six things, all fixed.

The measurement engine built

Exact integer arithmetic end to end — lengths in nanometres, areas in doubled square-nanometres, money in cents. No float ever touches a measurement.

Every dimension carries whether it was scanned, measured, derived or adjusted, and the app refuses to call a room measured until a tape has been on one wall running each way.

The business half built

Takeoff, price book, proposal with e-signature and audit trail, signed baseline, change orders, scheduling into the phone's calendar, invoicing, QuickBooks export.

This is the half magicplan does not have, and it is the whole pricing argument.

Insurance restoration built

Damage pins dropped live during a scan, photographs with the camera pose attached, the claim document as a real PDF, and ESX export for Xactimate.

The research names ESX as magicplan's strongest moat and the reason restoration contractors pay for it.

Crash and error reporting built

Apple’s own MetricKit for native crashes and hangs, and window.onerror for everything the web screens throw — which MetricKit cannot see at all, and which is most of this app.

Both written to a folder on the phone. Nothing is sent until somebody taps Send them. No server, no third-party SDK, nothing about the job.

Never been sold unproven

Zero customers. Zero App Store listing. Zero analytics — deliberately, and permanently: that is what “nothing leaves the device” costs. See section 06.

The one thing the research proves you own

Nobody sells interiors + accuracy + estimating in one product. Hover owns exteriors. magicplan owns insurance restoration. Polycam owns raw 3D. CubiCasa owns real-estate volume. The remodeler doing interior as-builts to price a job is served by pieces of all of them and the whole of none.

And Matterport — the most famous name in the category — explicitly says its floor plans are “not CAD-accurate drawings and should not be used for construction or renovation planning.” The category leader disclaims your use case in writing.

03

The price, and the argument against it

You are asking 2.4× magicplan with no brand and no track record

magicplan is ~$33.33/mo flat and already does LiDAR scanning, floor plans, estimates and ESX export to Xactimate. It has years of restoration contractors using it. You are proposing $78/mo with zero customers and no name.

If your pitch is “a better scanner,” you lose this argument on the first call. Nobody pays 2.4× for a better version of the thing they already have.

The argument that does work

You are not selling a scanner. You are selling a scanner plus the job-management half, and that half is normally a second subscription. Jobber — scheduling, quoting, invoicing — is a separate product a contractor buys alongside magicplan.

So the honest comparison is not $78 against $33. It is $78 against magicplan plus a job-management tool, bought separately, that do not share a number between them — where every quantity gets retyped from one into the other, by hand, at eleven at night.

That retyping is your actual product. The takeoff, the proposal, the change order and the invoice all come off the same measured walls, and when a tape moves a wall, every one of them moves with it. That cannot be replicated by two products next to each other at any price.

What that means for how you sell it

  • do
    Lead with the change order.

    A wall moved, the proposal was signed, and the app produced a priced change order off the difference. That is a sentence a restoration contractor feels in his stomach, because unbilled change orders are where his money goes.

  • do
    Lead with the refusal.

    Show it saying these are the scanner’s numbers, not measurements. Every competitor hands over a number with no provenance. Yours is the only one that admits what it does not know, and an adjuster is the exact person who cares.

  • never
    Never lead with accuracy.

    You have no measured accuracy figure. The research doc says so in writing: no accuracy claim in it was produced on a device you own. Claiming one is the fastest way to be caught.

The gate, as it is built

+ 2 rooms kept at once

The entire measurement product. Somebody who only wants a floor plan never has to pay, and never will.

Paid

takeoff · pricing · proposal · signature
change orders · insurance · exports
price list · unlimited rooms

Everything that turns a drawing into money.

The risk in that line, said plainly

The free tier is the whole reason a remodeler tells another remodeler about it — which is the distribution you are counting on. It is also the reason most of them never pay: a one-man shop who wants a floor plan for a bathroom has everything he needs, free, forever.

That is a deliberate trade and it is the right one. But it means your conversion rate is not a funnel number you can optimise — it is the fraction of your users who actually write proposals and file claims. Set expectations there accordingly.

What those numbers are and are not

Net a month and a customer is worth are arithmetic on your inputs, not forecasts. Churn is the one number that decides whether this is a business, and you do not have it and cannot get it until people have been paying for six months. Everything downstream of it is a guess wearing a decimal point.

06

The case where it fails

You asked for this one. It is not the tail risk; it is the base rate.

1 · The free tier is the product most likely

Remodelers love it, tell each other about it, and never pay — because a floor plan is all they wanted. You get a thousand users, a great App Store rating, and $400 a month.

The tell: installs climbing while paid conversions stay flat for two months.

2 · Restoration will not switch plausible

They already have magicplan, it already does ESX, and their Xactimate workflow works. Switching costs a busy contractor a week he does not have, to save nothing he can name.

The tell: demos go well and nobody buys. “Send me something” twice.

3 · Churn eats it plausible

At 10% a month, a customer lasts ten months. You spend every month replacing the people who left, and the count never moves no matter how good the product is.

The tell: gross adds healthy, net adds near zero by month four.

4 · You are half blind was certain

When it breaks you will now hear about it: MetricKit for crashes and hangs, and the web half catching everything these screens throw, both written to the phone with one tap to send them. Fixed 26 August 2026.

When somebody stops using it you still will not, and never will — there is no analytics and there is not going to be, because that is the price of the privacy promise. There is no tell for this one. Test 4 in section 09 is the substitute.

The honest summary

The product is unusually good and unusually far along for a business with zero customers. That is the trap. Every remaining risk is commercial, and none of it is fixed by more building — which is the thing you are good at and the thing that will feel productive.

The cheapest way to find out whether this is a business is to try to charge one restoration contractor $78 before you write another feature.

07

Launch, gate by gate

In order. Each one blocks the next; none of them is optional; several take days of waiting rather than work.

  1. Walk Gilbert’s kitchen

    Before anything else on this list. docs/on-the-phone.md has 22 tests and not one has been run on a phone. If the scan is wrong in a real kitchen, nothing below matters.

  2. Buy the identity you need on scantobid.app

    Apple's listing requires a support URL and a privacy policy URL, both live before submission. Two pages. Changing them later means a new review.

  3. Set the products in App Store Connect

    The prices are not in the code — StoreKit reads them from App Store Connect, which is correct. com.sunnyacres.trueline.pro.monthly and .pro.yearly have to be created, priced at $78 and $780, and submitted. Both need a screenshot and a review note.

  4. TestFlight, with Gilbert on it

    Internal first, then Gilbert externally. External TestFlight needs its own review, which is usually a day but is not instant.

  5. The privacy nutrition label

    Yours is the easy version and it is a genuine asset: no data collected, nothing leaves the device. Say it exactly, because it is unusual and it is true.

  6. Screenshots that show the refusal

    Six screens. Make one of them the app saying these are the scanner’s numbers, not measurements. Nobody else’s store page can show that.

  7. Submit, and expect one rejection

    Subscription apps are commonly rejected first time over paywall clarity: the price, the period, and what auto-renews have to be visible on the paywall itself. Yours already says the founding terms out loud, which helps.

09

What has to be true

Each of these is falsifiable and cheap to test. In this order, because each one makes the next worth doing.

  • test 1
    The scan is right in a real kitchen.

    Gilbert’s. Tape four walls yourself and compare. One afternoon. If this fails nothing else on this page is worth reading.

  • test 2
    A restoration contractor will sit through the change-order demo.

    Not buy — watch. If you cannot hold five minutes of attention on the strongest thing the product does, the positioning is wrong, not the price.

  • test 3
    One of them will pay $78 before the App Store listing exists.

    Invoice them directly. If somebody will pay for a TestFlight build with no store page and no reviews, you have a business. If nobody will, the store page will not change that.

  • test 4
    They are still paying in month three.

    The only churn number you will ever have that is not a guess. Everything in section 05 hangs on it.

10

The next thirty days

  1. Days 1–2 — Gilbert’s kitchen

    Build to the phone, walk it, run tests 2 and 20. Fix whatever the real room breaks.

  2. Days 3–5 — two web pages on scantobid.app

    Support and privacy. They gate the submission and they take an afternoon.

  3. Days 6–10 — App Store Connect

    Products at $78 and $780, screenshots, privacy label, TestFlight to Gilbert.

  4. Days 10–20 — five restoration contractors

    Not a launch. Five conversations, in person, with the change-order demo. Ask each of them to pay. Write down the exact words they use when they say no.

  5. Days 20–30 — act on what they said

    If three of five said the same thing, that is your roadmap and it beats everything either of us has guessed on this page.
