The ScanToBid Wedge

Everybody sells a scanner, or an estimating platform. Nobody sells the seam between them — and the seam is where a remodeler loses 30 to 45 minutes on every bid he writes.

The one thing this is

If only one sentence survives this document, it should be this one.

Every product in this market sits on one side of a gap. Scanners — magicplan, Polycam, CubiCasa, Matterport, Hover — turn a room into a drawing and stop. Estimating platforms — Buildertrend, JobTread, Houzz Pro, Contractor Foreman — turn a list of quantities into a price and a proposal, and have no idea where the quantities came from. Between them sits a man at a kitchen table at nine at night, typing numbers off one screen into another.

The trade press names the cost of that gap directly: the double entry between takeoff and pricing runs 30 to 45 minutes per bid, and it produces arithmetic errors on the way through. That is not a missing feature. That is the entire product.

ScanToBid is the only thing that measures and prices in one act — and the only one that refuses to let a number pretend it was measured when it was not. That refusal looks like a limitation until the first time a homeowner asks where a figure came from.

Where the money actually leaks

The four you named, each against what the trade reports, and what ScanToBid does about it today.

Leak 01 — the clock

Hours at a kitchen table turning a tape and a notepad into a number, after a day already worked. The bid goes out late, and sometimes not at all — the job is lost to whoever answered first, not to whoever priced better.

BUILT · The takeoff is derived from the room the moment it closes. No re-entry, no second tool, no arithmetic done by hand.
Leak 02 — the winner’s curse

The industry’s own name for it: scope gets missed, the bid comes in low, and you win the job because the estimate was incomplete. Estimating around a missing sheet is quoted as the fastest way to underbid by 15%.

BUILT · Every dimension carries where it came from, and the room refuses to call itself measured until a tape has been on one wall running each way.
Leak 03 — scope creep

Work that grew, nothing signed, no clear record of what the original scope was. The platforms that compete hardest here — JobTread, Buildertrend, Leap — all compete on change orders tied to a budget, not on measuring.

MISSING · There is no signed baseline, so there is nothing for a change to be a change from. This is the largest hole in the product.
Leak 04 — looking amateur

Losing bids to someone with better paperwork rather than better numbers. Vendors claim a digital, signable proposal closes materially better than a handwritten one — treat their figures as marketing, but the direction is not in dispute.

PART BUILT · The drawing, the client file and the claim document exist and are good. Nothing can be signed, and there is no priced proposal.

Two things fall out of that table. First, every serious platform charges per seat per month and every scanner charges per scan or per project — and per-scan pricing is repeatedly called out as punishing exactly the small remodeler this is for. A flat seat price is a real weapon.

Second, and more important: the measurement is the front door. Hover built a whole business on being the thing that measures and then handing the result to fifty other tools. ScanToBid is already the best-measuring thing in this list. It has almost none of the doors.

What to build, in this order

Ordered by money recovered per week of work. The numbering is the build order and nothing else — each one is worth doing on its own.

  1. Size mediumFixes leak 04Needs no integration

    The signable proposal

    The takeoff already knows the quantities and the price book already knows the rates. What is missing is the document a homeowner says yes to: scope in plain sentences, the drawing, the price, terms, and a signature taken on the phone in the kitchen. Good / better / best as three columns, because the trade is unanimous that options outsell a single number.

    This is first because it needs nothing from anybody else — no account system, no third-party API — and it is the only item here that directly wins work rather than saving time.

  2. Size mediumFixes leak 03Depends on 01

    The signed baseline, and change orders against it

    The moment a proposal is signed, freeze it. Every later edit to the room — a wall moved, an opening added, a rate changed — becomes a diff against that frozen scope, priced, with its own signature line. Scope creep stops being an argument about memory and becomes a list.

    This is the single largest hole in the product and the thing the expensive platforms actually compete on. It is also nearly free once 01 exists, because the ledger discipline for it is already in the codebase: nothing is overwritten, and provenance is carried per dimension.

  3. Size smallFixes leak 01Unblocks everything

    More than one room in a job

    A remodel is a kitchen and a hallway and two baths. Today a scan is a room and a job is a room, so a whole-house estimate is four files and a calculator — which puts the 30-to-45-minute problem straight back.

    Small only because the room model is clean enough to nest. Listed third rather than first because a proposal for one room is still a proposal, and a signed baseline for one room still stops an argument.

  4. Size smallFixes leaks 02, 04Half built

    Photos that file themselves

    Damage photographs already attach to a wall. Generalise it: any photo, stamped with the job, the room, the wall, the date and which way the phone was pointing — because the room already knows where north is. Then a before-and-after sheet costs one tap.

    CompanyCam is a hundred-thousand-contractor business built on exactly this and nothing else. ScanToBid can do something it cannot: file a photograph against a wall, not just a job.

  5. Size mediumFixes leak 01Recurring revenue

    Doors out — one interface, several destinations

    You are not sure Plumbline gets finished, so nothing should assume it. Build one export interface and hang the destinations off it: a CSV a spreadsheet reads, a QuickBooks-shaped invoice, the ESX for Xactimate that already exists, a plain PDF for reading off, and Plumbline later as one more implementation rather than the point.

    Every destination added makes the app harder to leave. That is the entire Hover playbook.

  6. Size largeFixes leak 02Needs real history

    Prices that come from your own finished jobs

    Not an AI guess and not a national average: what you actually got paid, on jobs like this one, in this town. Once a job is priced, signed and closed, that is a data point. Fifty of them and the app can tell a contractor his tile rate is a year out of date.

    Last because it needs a history that does not exist yet, and because it is worthless until 01 and 02 are producing signed, closed jobs to learn from.

Decisions only you can make

Each of these changes what gets built. None of them should be guessed.

Does a signature have to hold up legally?

A picture of a finger-drawn squiggle and a genuine audit-trailed e-signature are different products. The second means a third party, a monthly cost, and a privacy story. The first is honest if it is never called more than it is.

Does anything leave the phone?

Everything is local today, and the app says so. Accounts, sync, a customer portal and a shared job all need a server — which means a bill, a backup policy, and somebody's homeowner data in your care.

One trade or all of them?

The interior remodeler is a real, underserved wedge. Roofing, flooring and restoration each have their own vocabulary and their own incumbent. Winning one properly beats being third in four.

What does it cost, and who is billed?

A flat seat price is the competitive weapon here. But nothing has been built for billing, and an App Store subscription is Apple's 15–30% plus a review process that will look hard at a measurement claim.

What I could not verify

  • No price here was quoted to me. All of them come from trade comparison articles, several of which are published by competitors of the products they compare. Treat them as the right order of magnitude, not as quotes.
  • The e-signature close-rate figures are vendor marketing. They appear in material published by companies selling e-signatures. I have not found an independent study, and I am not going to repeat the percentages as if I had.
  • No accuracy figure in this document was measured by me. There is no LiDAR device and no Swift compiler in the environment this was written in.
  • I have not spoken to a contractor. Everything about what Gilbert needs is inference from you, from published complaints, and from the shape of what the market sells. One afternoon watching him write a bid would be worth more than this entire page.
