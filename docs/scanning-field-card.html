Field card — scanning a room
Rev 1 · for iPhone / iPad LiDAR

A scan is only worth what the walk was worth. A room walked wrong cannot be fixed afterwards — it has to be walked again.

01Where to stand

4–6 ft
Off the wall — aim here
3–15 ft
Usable band. Edges drift
16 ft
Range ceiling · 5 m

Why 4–6 ft: the whole wall run has to be in frame, including where it meets the floor. Hug the wall and the sensor only ever sees a patch — no long straight edge to fit a plane to. Stand too far back in a big room and the far wall is past range. Minimum working distance is ~8–10 in.

02How to hold it

HeightChest height, tilted slightly down.
AimSo the camera sees where the wall meets the floor. That joint is the strongest signal the scanner has for finding the room's outline. Pointing level at blank drywall gives it almost nothing to work with.
GripTwo hands. Elbows in. Smooth beats fast.

03How to walk it

  1. Start in a corner, never mid-wall. Starting mid-wall slows the tracker's orientation and increases drift from the first second.
  2. Move your body, not your arms. Walk the perimeter at a slow walking pace, sweeping gently side to side, camera angled inward toward the middle of the room. Standing still and panning is what produces drift.
  3. Pause a beat at every corner. Corners are where the geometry gets pinned down. Rushing them is the most common cause of a room that will not close.
  4. Finish where you started. Returning to known ground is what lets the tracker correct everything it accumulated on the way round. This is the single biggest quality lever on the card.
  5. Turn the lights on. LiDAR does not care about darkness. The camera tracking that stitches it all together does.

04The limits — these are where it breaks

Under 5 minPer room. Longer sessions drift, the device heats up, and tracking degrades.
Under 30 × 30 ftApple's recommended maximum scan area. Bigger spaces get split into separate scans.
One room per scanDo not try to walk the house in one go. Each room is its own capture.
Glass & mirrorsReflective surfaces cut reliability. Note where they are rather than trusting them.
If the app tells you something, it wins RoomPlan gives live feedback — "Move farther away", "Slow down", "More light". It is reading your actual session; this card is not. Follow the screen over this page, every time.
Figures are published manufacturer and industry values, not measurements taken by Trueline. Range ~5 m / 16 ft; minimum working distance ~8–10 in; recommended max scan area ~9 × 9 m. Validate against your own device before quoting any of them to a customer.

05The tape log — the most valuable thing you do

A scan on its own has no ground truth. Tape three or four walls per room and write the numbers here — it turns "accurate within a published range" into "accurate within a figure we measured."

RoomWhich wallTape saysScan saysNotes
Measure the same wall twice Once at the start of the scan and once at the end. If the two readings differ, that is drift — a different problem from sensor error, and it gets fixed differently.

06Also worth writing down

Scan time per room — bumping five minutes on a normal room explains a lot of drift on its own. Which device, and whether more than one was used on the same room. What it got visibly wrong — a double door read as one, a closet swallowed, a wall split in two, an angled wall squared off. Those are the failure modes worth catching now rather than in six months.

07When it goes wrong

What happenedWhat to do
The room will not closeWalk it again in the opposite direction before giving up. Known ground from a new angle lets the tracker correct itself.
A wall is missing entirelyToo close, or held level rather than tilted down. Re-walk it at 4–6 ft, camera angled at the floor joint.
Tracking jumped or resetStop and start the room again. A scan that lost tracking is not worth correcting afterwards.
Device got hotScan was too long. Split the space up and let the device cool.

08Scan the simple room first
